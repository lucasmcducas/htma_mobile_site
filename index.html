What it reads

A snapshot tells you what is moving. A chart tells you what is stored.

Hair tissue mineral analysis records deposition over roughly three months of growth. Nothing about it is a snapshot — which is what makes the ratios on the chart worth having explained to you.

What it does

Four things, done properly.

A chart with forty numbers on it is not information. The app finds the patterns, tells you which ones are worth acting on, and keeps tracking them across retests.

01
Read your chart

Upload a report from any lab, or have one sent to you directly. No re-keying forty values by hand.

02
See the patterns

Ratio patterns like Four Lows and adrenal or thyroid groupings, flagged in plain language rather than mineral shorthand.

03
Follow a protocol

Recommendations drawn from your own results, with supplement doses and a day-to-day plan you can actually keep.

04
Send it on

Share the report with your practitioner in one tap, and compare side by side when you retest.

Why hair

Blood is a photograph. Hair is a film.

Blood shows what is in circulation right now, and your body works hard to keep those numbers steady by pulling from tissue. Hair records what was actually stored.

Blood

Measures what is moving at one moment. Read a different value on a different day without anything about you having changed.

A snapshot can look fine while the pattern underneath it is not.

Hair

Records deposition across roughly three months of growth. The ratios are the point, and they need someone who knows how to read them.

That is what the app is for — and what your practitioner is for.

The method

Forty years old. Not a trend.

Hair tissue mineral analysis was pioneered by Dr. Paul Eck and refined by Dr. Lawrence Wilson. The app is a reading tool for that methodology — it does not replace the person who ordered your test.
